# ADR for Dummies: "Who it's for" on the homepage

- **Status:** Accepted 2026-10-22
- **Date:** 2026-10-22

## The Idea

Under the hero, the homepage has two rows of scrolling chips ("SaaS billing", "Marketplace fees"…). They name a kind of business but don't say why it should care. We replace them with a short "Who it's for" section.

## What the Person Sees

1. A heading, "Who it's for", and five tabs: SaaS, Marketplaces, Creators, Agencies, Global B2B.
2. The open tab shows one line on the problem that kind of business has, one line on how Strimz solves it, and a picture of that feature (the same one as on the use-cases page).
3. Clicking another tab fades the panel over to it.
4. Each panel has a link, for example "See how agencies use Strimz", which jumps to that story on the use-cases page.
5. On a phone, the tabs sit in a row you can swipe, with the panel below.
6. Keyboard users move between tabs with the arrow keys.

## What Changes

- The scrolling chips are gone.
- The five audiences, their problems and their fixes live in one list, which the menu, homepage and use-cases page all share.

## What Does Not Change

- The use-cases page looks the same.
- No "Sample" or "Live" labels, no network name, no comparison with other payment companies.
- No DAO entry, because the product doesn't target DAOs today.
