---
title: How Strimz works
description: What happens between creating a payment and the money landing in your wallet.
---

You talk to Strimz through the API, the SDKs or the dashboard. Everything else, from the wallet prompts to the network fee to the webhook, Strimz handles for you.

## A one-time payment

1. **You create a payment session** (or a payment link) with an amount and a currency. Strimz returns a checkout URL.
2. **Your customer opens the hosted checkout** and connects a wallet. They approve the payment with signatures, not a transaction, so they don't need gas.
3. **Strimz submits the payment on Arc** and pays the network fee.
4. **The money lands in your payout wallet** in the same transaction, minus the Strimz fee. Strimz never holds it.
5. **Strimz records the payment** in your dashboard and sends `payment.completed` to your webhook endpoint, with the on-chain transaction hash.

## A subscription

1. **Your customer subscribes once** in the hosted checkout. They approve the plan's amount and schedule with a signature.
2. **Strimz charges them on each billing date,** straight from their wallet to yours. They don't have to come back.
3. **If a charge fails,** for example because their balance is low, Strimz retries and emails them. You get webhooks for each step: `subscription.charge_failed` when a charge fails, `subscription.charged` when one succeeds and, if it never recovers within the grace window, `subscription.lapsed`.

Either side can cancel at any time. See [Subscriptions](/docs/subscriptions/lifecycle).

## Test mode and live mode

Everything above works the same in both modes. Test mode uses free test USDC on Arc testnet; live mode moves real money on Arc mainnet. See [Test mode and live mode](/docs/testing).

## What you don't need to think about

<Callout title="Built-in guarantees" type="info">

- **On-chain idempotency.** The contract's `usedAttempts[id]` mapping makes a charge attempt safe to replay: a reused id is skipped with outcome `Duplicate`, never charged twice.
- **Webhook delivery retries.** Strimz makes 5 attempts, waiting 1m, 5m, 30m and 2h between them. If all five fail, you get an email, and 5 permanent failures in 24 hours auto-disable the endpoint.
- **Finality.** Arc has deterministic sub-second finality, so a confirmed payment stays confirmed. There are no reorgs to wait out.

</Callout>
