---
title: Resources
description: The objects you'll interact with. What they mean, when they exist, what their lifecycles look like.
---

Strimz exposes a small number of resources. Most have a lifecycle of statuses that's worth knowing before you start wiring webhooks.

## Merchant

You, basically. Created by Privy on first login. Holds business info
(name, sector, country, payout address) and configuration (active
capabilities, agent settings). Most other resources hang off
`merchantId`.

## Customer

Anyone who has paid you. Strimz creates a `Customer` row automatically the first time a wallet pays one of your sessions or subscriptions. Unique on `(merchantId, walletAddress)`. The same wallet across two merchants is two distinct rows.

## PaymentSession

A one-shot checkout. You create one with an amount, currency, and (optional) description. Strimz returns a `checkoutUrl`. The customer pays. The session goes through:

```
created  →  awaiting_payment  →  submitted  →  confirmed
                    ↘  expired           ↘  failed
```

A session can also be `cancelled`. Sessions expire after 30 minutes by default; set `expiresInMinutes` (up to 1440) to change that. A session still `created` or `awaiting_payment` at its expiry moves to `expired`, fires `payment.failed`, and can no longer be paid. Refunds are tracked on the transaction, not as a session status.

## Subscription + SubscriptionPlan

A `SubscriptionPlan` is the _template_ (name, amount, interval, intervalCount). A `Subscription` is the _instance_: a specific customer subscribed to a specific plan.

```
trialing  →  active  →  at_risk  →  lapsed
                 ↓          ↓
             cancelled   cancelled
```

A failed charge moves a sub from `active` to `at_risk`, and the scheduler retries on a backoff. If the recovery agent is on, it emails the customer per your strategy. If no charge succeeds within 48 hours of the period end, the sub flips to `lapsed` and stops attempting charges. See [Subscription lifecycle](/docs/subscriptions/lifecycle). The customer can also cancel directly from their wallet. The indexer projects `SubscriptionCancelled` and Strimz marks the row.

## SubscriptionCharge

One charge attempt. The scheduler sends the charge when a sub is due, and the indexer writes the row from the contract's event. Idempotency comes from `chargeAttemptId = keccak256(abi.encodePacked(onchainSubId, currentPeriodEndAt, attempt))`; the contract skips a reused id with outcome `duplicate`.

```
pending  →  succeeded  ↦  Transaction(kind=subscription_charge)
       ↘   failed (with outcome enum)
```

## Transaction

A confirmed on-chain payment. There are several kinds:

| `kind`                | Source                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `one_shot`            | Customer paid a session              |
| `subscription_charge` | Scheduler successfully charged a sub |
| `refund`              | A refund tx was confirmed            |

`Transaction.amount` is in smallest token units (USDC has 6 decimals). `feeAmount` is the Strimz platform fee at the merchant's tier. `netAmount` is what landed in the merchant's payout wallet.

## Refund

Server-side intent, wallet-signed transfer.

```
awaiting_signature  →  submitted  →  completed
                                ↘  failed
```

You create the refund. Strimz validates the amount against
`Transaction.amount` so cumulative refunds cannot exceed the
original. On the dashboard, your Strimz wallet then signs and sends
the ERC-20 transfer. The indexer sees the on-chain transfer and
flips the row to `completed`.

`strimz.refunds.create` returns `{ refund, signingInstructions }`:
the refund and the transfer to sign.

## Invoice

A bill with a number, line items and a due date, backed by a `PaymentSession`. Sending it emails the buyer the breakdown and a link to that session's hosted checkout. There is no separate hosted invoice page; a PDF is available from the dashboard.

```
draft  →  sent  →  paid
            ↘   overdue
```

Any unpaid invoice can be moved to `void`. A `sent` invoice that passes its `dueAt` unpaid flips to `overdue` (hourly cron) and fires `invoice.overdue`. An overdue invoice can still be paid.

## WebhookEndpoint + WebhookDelivery

An `Endpoint` is a URL + event subscription list + signing secret. A `Delivery` is one attempt to POST a specific event to a specific endpoint:

```
pending  →  delivered                      (2xx response)
        ↘   retrying  →  permanently_failed (after max attempts)
```

Every delivery is one BullMQ job. The attempts stay visible in your dashboard so you can debug a flaky receiver row by row.

## AgentJob

ERC-8183 escrow job. Used by the AutoPay Agent's commerce capability to pay vendors via on-chain escrow. Agent escrow (the `commerce` capability) is coming soon and not available at launch.

```
proposed  →  accepted  →  in_progress  →  delivered  →  approved  →  completed
                                                 ↘   disputed / cancelled
```

`proposed` means it's awaiting human approval (above your configured threshold). `accepted` auto-fires below threshold.
