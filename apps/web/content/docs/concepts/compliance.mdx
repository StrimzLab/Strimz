title: Compliance
description: 'What stops a blocklisted wallet from paying through Strimz today, and what Strimz does not screen yet.'
---

Stablecoin payments are still payments. They're subject to OFAC's sanctions list and the country-level sanctions regimes that follow. This page says exactly what protects a Strimz payment today and what doesn't, so you can decide what to layer on top.

## What runs today

Strimz does not run third-party wallet screening. No payer or merchant wallet is sent to a screening provider, and no risk score is computed.

What does stop a sanctioned wallet is the chain itself. Arc enforces Circle's USDC blocklist in the protocol:

- A transaction from a blocklisted sender is rejected before it reaches the mempool.
- A USDC `transfer` or `transferFrom` where the sender or the recipient is blocklisted reverts.

See [Arc's blocklist compliance guide](https://docs.arc.io/integrate/infrastructure/compliance).

Strimz simulates every relayed payment before it submits it. When the payer is on Circle's blocklist, the simulation fails, the relay answers `400` with `code: 'relay_simulation_failed'`, and nothing is broadcast, so no gas is spent.

This covers wallets on Circle's blocklist. It does not cover wallets that are high risk but not blocklisted; those pay normally.

## What isn't live yet

The API contains adapters for TRM Labs and Elliptic, switched off. When a provider is turned on, a provider error counts as a block: Strimz would rather refuse a payment than settle one it could not screen. Screening at checkout is not wired in yet, and we'll update this page and the changelog before it is.

A few published names exist for that future and are not sent today:

- The webhook events `compliance.wallet_flagged` and `compliance.wallet_blocked`.
- The SDK error code `compliance_blocked`.

Don't build a flow that waits for them yet. The read-only `GET /v1/compliance/logs` endpoint returns an empty list while screening is off.

## What it doesn't cover

Even once screening is on, a few things are out of scope:

- It doesn't do KYC on the payer. Strimz doesn't know who's behind a wallet; we know the wallet address.
- It doesn't do AML investigation. If a wallet is moving suspicious amounts to and from your business, we don't surface that.
- It doesn't do travel-rule data exchange.
- It doesn't do tax reporting.

If you have a regulated use case that needs more than this, such as a money services business or anything that does customer due diligence, layer your own KYC on top of Strimz. We give you the wallet address; you can hook a Persona, Sumsub or similar flow at signup.

Questions about a specific sector or jurisdiction go to [compliance@strimz.finance](mailto:compliance@strimz.finance).
