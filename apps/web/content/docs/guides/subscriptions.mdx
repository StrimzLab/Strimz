---
title: Build a subscription
description: Recurring stablecoin billing. The customer signs once at checkout and the scheduler charges each period.
---

Strimz subscriptions are pull-based. Your customer signs once at
checkout to let the contract charge a fixed amount on a fixed schedule.
After that, the Strimz scheduler takes each charge when it is due, with
no further prompts.

## Define a plan

```ts
const plan = await strimz.subscriptionPlans.create({
  name: 'Pro',
  amount: '20000000', // 20 USDC, 6 decimals
  currency: 'USDC',
  interval: 'monthly',
  intervalCount: 1,
  trialPeriodDays: 14,
})

const checkoutUrl = `https://www.strimz.finance/sub/${plan.id}`
```

Plans are templates. They don't represent a single customer; they represent a recurring price the customer can subscribe to. The plan object has no checkout URL field, so build the link from `plan.id`.

## Customer subscribes

Send the customer to the checkout link. They connect a wallet, see
"Subscribe, 20 USDC/month", and sign two typed-data messages:

1. An EIP-2612 `Permit` that gives the `StrimzSubscriptions` contract
   an allowance on their USDC.
2. A Strimz `SubscriptionIntent` that binds the plan, amount, interval
   and start time they agreed to.

Strimz's relayer submits both in one `permitAndCreateSubscription`
transaction. That transaction creates the subscription and moves no
money.

The full security model lives on the
[meta-tx flow page](/docs/concepts/meta-tx-flow): what the customer
signs, why the relayer cannot move funds on its own, when the
signature expires.

You receive `subscription.created` once the indexer sees the
enrolment, and `subscription.charged` each time a period charges.

## What happens at charge time

The Strimz scheduler runs a sweep every 15 minutes. It finds subscriptions whose `nextChargeAt` (and any retry time) has passed, locks each one with `chargeLock`, and enqueues one job per subscription onto `strimz.subscription.due`.

The worker calls `batchCharge([subscriptionId], [chargeAttemptId])` for that one subscription. The attempt id is `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(onchainSubId, currentPeriodEndAt, attempt))`, so a replayed attempt can't charge twice: the contract skips it with outcome `Duplicate`. The contract pulls USDC from the customer's wallet and emits `SubscriptionCharged`, or `SubscriptionChargeSkipped` with the reason. The indexer projects both. See [How charges fire](/docs/subscriptions/charging).

## Handle a failed charge

A payment failure moves the subscription to `at_risk` and fires `subscription.charge_failed`. The payload is `{ subscription, charge }`, and `charge.outcome` is one of:

| `outcome`            | What it means                                               |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `insufficient_funds` | Customer's balance is below the charge amount               |
| `revoked_approval`   | Customer's allowance to the contract is too low             |
| `transfer_failed`    | The token transfer failed, for example a blocklisted wallet |
| `merchant_inactive`  | Your merchant account is not active in the registry         |

Other outcomes, such as `not_due` or `duplicate`, are recorded but don't fire a webhook.

The scheduler retries after 15 minutes, 1 hour, 3 hours, 6 hours, 12 hours, then every 24 hours. If the charge still hasn't succeeded 48 hours after the period end, an hourly cron flips the subscription to `lapsed` and fires `subscription.lapsed`. If you've enabled the agent's `recovery` capability, the customer also gets emails. See [Recovery](/docs/subscriptions/recovery).

## Cancel a subscription

There are two paths to cancellation. Both are immediate.

**From your server.** Say the customer's account was deleted on
your side:

```ts
await strimz.subscriptions.cancel({ id: subscriptionId, reason: 'merchant initiated' })
```

The row becomes `cancelled` straight away, `subscription.cancelled`
fires during the call, and the scheduler stops charging. The API also
queues an on-chain cancel, which works from the next testnet redeploy
([#142](https://github.com/StrimzLab/strimz/issues/142)) and reverts on the current deployment.

**From the customer's wallet.** The customer calls `cancel()` on the
contract directly. The indexer picks up `SubscriptionCancelled` and
flips the row. No webhook fires for this path today.

Either way, the customer stops getting charged. Their token allowance
still exists. To remove it, the customer calls `approve(spender, 0)`
on the USDC contract.

## Trial periods

`trialPeriodDays: 14` on the plan means the first charge is due 14 days after enrolment instead of at enrolment. The customer signs immediately; Strimz takes nothing until the trial ends. A payer gets the trial only on their first subscription to that plan.

Without a trial, the first charge is due at enrolment and the scheduler takes it on its next sweep, within about 15 minutes.

## Pricing math

Strimz charges a per-transaction fee on every charge based on your tier. For a Starter (0.5%) merchant subscribing a customer to a 20 USDC monthly plan:

- Customer pays: **20.00 USDC**
- Strimz fee: **0.10 USDC** (0.5%)
- Merchant receives: **19.90 USDC**

The fee is taken on chain, in the same transaction as the charge, by `StrimzSubscriptions`. There's no separate invoicing. Your dashboard's MRR view shows both gross and net.
