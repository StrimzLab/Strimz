---
title: Recovery
description: When a charge fails, Strimz retries with backoff for up to 48 hours past the period end before the subscription lapses.
---

When a charge fails, the subscription moves to `at_risk` and the scheduler retries it on a backoff schedule. If a retry succeeds, the subscription goes back to `active`. If the 48-hour grace window runs out first, it lapses.

## What triggers recovery

The contract skips a charge with one of these outcomes:

- `insufficient_funds`: the payer's balance is below the charge amount. The most common cause.
- `revoked_approval`: the payer's allowance to the subscriptions contract is too low, often because they set it to zero on purpose.
- `transfer_failed`: the token transfer failed, for example because the token blocklisted the payer's address.
- `merchant_inactive`: the merchant is not active in the registry.

The contract emits `SubscriptionChargeSkipped` instead of `SubscriptionCharged`. The indexer writes a failed `SubscriptionCharge` row, fires `subscription.charge_failed`, and flips the subscription to `at_risk`. It only does that when the period has no successful charge yet (see the [race-condition guard](/docs/concepts/safety-nets#the-race-condition-projector-fix)).

Not yet: a failed charge while the subscription is `trialing` does not move it to `at_risk` or schedule a retry. Tracked in [#295](https://github.com/StrimzLab/strimz/issues/295).

## The retry schedule

When the indexer records a failed charge, it sets the next retry time:

| Failure       | Next retry after |
| ------------- | ---------------- |
| 1st           | 15 minutes       |
| 2nd           | 1 hour           |
| 3rd           | 3 hours          |
| 4th           | 6 hours          |
| 5th           | 12 hours         |
| 6th and later | 24 hours         |

The sweeper runs every 15 minutes, so a retry fires on the first run after its time. The scheduler makes at most 8 attempts per billing period.

Not yet: when the payer's balance or allowance is still too low, the retry reuses the same attempt id, so the backoff doesn't advance and retries run on every sweep. Only the first failure sends `subscription.charge_failed`. Tracked in [#297](https://github.com/StrimzLab/strimz/issues/297).

## The grace window

`gracePeriodHours` is 48 on every subscription. It is not configurable through the API, SDK or dashboard.

An hourly scheduler cron moves every `at_risk` subscription to `lapsed` once `currentPeriodEndAt + 48 hours` has passed, and fires `subscription.lapsed`. In practice the schedule above fits about six retries inside the window.

The `recoveryGracePeriodHours` setting on the Agents page does not change this window. It only controls when recovery emails are sent.

## What fires on your webhook endpoint

- `subscription.charge_failed`: a failed charge was recorded. The payload is `{ subscription, charge }`; `charge.outcome` says why it failed.
- `subscription.charged`: a retry succeeded and the subscription is `active` again.
- `subscription.lapsed`: the grace window ran out. Use this to revoke access.

Not yet: `subscription.recovery_attempt` and `subscription.recovery_outcome` are listed as event types but are never sent. Tracked in [#295](https://github.com/StrimzLab/strimz/issues/295).

## Emailing the payer

The first `charge_failed` is the one to act on. Email the payer and ask them to top up their wallet. You can send it yourself from your webhook handler, or turn on the recovery agent on the [Agents page](/docs/dashboard/agents), which emails the customer if Strimz has their email on file.

## What it costs to retry

Each retry is an on-chain call. Strimz's scheduler pays the gas. You are not billed for failed retries.

There is no call to retry a charge on demand. The next retry follows the schedule above.

## After a lapse

Lapsed is terminal. Even if the payer tops up, Strimz will not charge the old subscription again.

To come back, the payer subscribes again on the plan's checkout link, which creates a new subscription with a new id. A payer can hold only one live subscription per plan; a lapsed one does not count, so they can re-subscribe straight away. The old row stays for audit.
